Paint the Town Red is a resource economy card, and it also allows you to pull out enemies from the encounter deck (e.g. to fish for a victory point enemy). It's a **Parley** event so you can use it with an enemy engaged without an attack of opportunity (though why you'd want to I don't know). It also has a <span class="icon-willpower"></span> and <span class="icon-agility"></span> icon, so it's somewhat useful to commit.

For me the only use case for this currently in Chapter 2 is Dexter, and only if you're just using the core box with no starter decks, and only if you're looking to target [Bat Horror](/card/12162). Dexter's assets tend to be expensive, so extra resources are appreciated, and with the Bat Horror's 4 health you get 4 resources (and then as it's elusive you can shoo it with an attack action). This gives 2 resources per action spent vs. [Emergency Cache](/card/12089)'s 3 (and even then, you need to not care about a Bat Horror running around for the remainder of the scenario...).

For most other 3 health enemies ([Hellhound](/card/12122) or [Mutated Experiment](/card/12132)) then you're then at 1.5 resources per action assuming you have [Shadowmeld](/card/60461) to get away from the enemy or upgraded [Cosmic Flame](/card/12071) to kill them in one - and even then I'd say keeping charges on these tends to be more valuable than netting 1 resource. Note for [Rogue Gangster](/card/12164) he will lose you a resource when he engages, so he's not a suitable target for this.

The <span class="icon-rogue"></span> resource tech in André's starter deck (e.g. [Out the Door](/card/60370), ["Watch this!"](/card/60371)), or even [Spiritual Charm](/card/60466) from Marie's deck if thinking of Dexter, make this totally and utterly redundant for me.

**Conclusions:** Aside the very (**<ins>very</ins>**) niche use-case I described, this is one of the most binder fodder-worthy cards I've ever seen! Generally new enemies = bad, and you should not be trying to draw them without a stronger incentive (e.g. [Stalk Prey](/card/60567) is an example of a good incentive). Unless FFG give us more incentives to pull enemies out of the encounter deck in later campaigns then I just don't see a use for this, and even then usually enemies like this come out of the encounter deck sooner or later without help. Easily the worst card in Chapter 2 for me, and it's not even close!

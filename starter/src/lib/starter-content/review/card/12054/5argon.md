Refer to the review of [<span class="fg-rogue">Sticky Fingers</span>](/card/12048) for its basic functions. For 2 XP, you've added :

1. "Fast" : This saves you one action (equivalent to 1 resource in action economy?), and lets you play it even after an enemy has engaged you. So if you are using your resources to pay for something else while waiting for an enemy to come, this keyword kind of "saves" resources, because you don't have to play it proactively.
2. <span class="icon-agility"></span><span class="icon-agility"></span> icons : These go together with how you can always keep this waiting in hand. You can pivot to commit <span class="icon-agility"></span><span class="icon-agility"></span> at any time, or commit the leftover copy if you double up on the card.

It is quite expensive XP-wise for these additional effects. But if you find you can't remove [<span class="fg-rogue">Sticky Fingers</span>](/card/12048) at all costs, it might look like a good refinement to your deck.

# Fast assets are played during your turn

Unlike the commonly seen Fast events, which always state their play timing, a Fast asset is simply "Fast." with no condition. (Also look at [<span class="fg-seeker">Magnifying Glass</span>](/card/12034), [<span class="fg-seeker">Magnifying Glass (1)</span>](/card/60270).) But the rules say any Fast **asset** can only be played **during your turn**. (Imagine it said the same as [<span class="fg-seeker">Working a Hunch</span>](/card/12038).) So, for example, even if you get a test in the Mythos Phase that would make you lose an asset in play after the test, you can't just put down [<span class="fg-rogue">Sticky Fingers (2)</span>](/card/12054) Fast there, because it's not your turn yet. Even in the Investigation Phase, you have to be the one currently taking a turn.

# Playing it during a skill test

If you are sure you are taking this test during your turn, you can play it in either of the two <span class="icon-free"></span> player windows around the commit step.

{{timing before 4.002 4.002 after 4.002}}

Currently I see no difference between doing this and just playing it before the test. I can think of one jank where [<span class="fg-rogue">Out the Door</span>](/card/60370) brings you up from 0 to 2 resources in the commit step. Then you Fast play [<span class="fg-rogue">Sticky Fingers (2)</span>](/card/12054) in the 2nd player window right after, taking you down to 1 resource. If you fail this test, you lose just 1 resource.

# [<span class="fg-mystic">Dexter Drake</span>](/card/12010)

You can perform some magic tricks with a Fast asset! Since he can't take [<span class="fg-seeker">Magnifying Glass</span>](/card/12034) / [<span class="fg-seeker">Magnifying Glass (1)</span>](/card/60270), [<span class="fg-rogue">Sticky Fingers (2)</span>](/card/12054) is the only Fast asset in the game he can take right now.

- To use his <span class="icon-reaction"></span>, you'd need to play an asset normally first, which provokes an AoO if you are already engaged, and takes an action too. [<span class="fg-rogue">Sticky Fingers (2)</span>](/card/12054) lets you play anything together with it, skipping the AoO altogether. [<span class="fg-mystic">Favor of Baalshandor</span>](/card/60463) can do a similar technique with its explicit AoO skipping, but it costs an action and 1 damage.
- If you use the 1st <span class="icon-free"></span> player window, before the ST.2 commit step, he can bounce up an asset he has in play and immediately commit that asset.
- If you use the 2nd <span class="icon-free"></span> player window within a test where you committed [<span class="fg-rogue">Out the Door</span>](/card/60370) / [<span class="fg-rogue">Out the Door (1)</span>](/card/60373), you can burn more resources on the 2nd asset played with the <span class="icon-reaction"></span>, so you use up the resources you would lose on a failure.
- By using either <span class="icon-free"></span> player window within the test to play [<span class="fg-rogue">Sticky Fingers (2)</span>](/card/12054), he can bounce back the asset he just initiated the test with. Whether this matters remains to be seen. For example, if [<span class="fg-mystic">Cosmic Flame (5)</span>](/card/12071) is at 0 charges and you are afraid of <span class="icon-skull"></span>, it seems that even if you bounce it up and then reveal <span class="icon-skull"></span>, reading literally, you would still take 1 damage AND discard it from your hand. (I'm not sure about this either, so I've asked the community.)

If you've used it for a while, think you are already rich, and no longer need the resource effect, it can be an asset bounce-up target, so you get <span class="icon-agility"></span><span class="icon-agility"></span> to commit.

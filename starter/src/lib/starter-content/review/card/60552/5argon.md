"As if". This phrase has brought countless games to a halt! You must throw away your logic when evaluating the result of doing it. Well, if you are not sure about a ruling, at least you can take the draw! Now, if you are ready to play with an "as if", this is the rule:

{{rule 1.012}}

An obviously safe and useful play would be laying down attachments at nearby locations without going there ([<span class="fg-survivor">Lie in Wait</span>](/card/60566), [<span class="fg-survivor">Glassing</span>](/card/60563), [<span class="fg-survivor">Decoy Trap</span>](/card/60562), [<span class="fg-survivor">Makeshift Bomb</span>](/card/60581), [<span class="fg-survivor">Rope Trap</span>](/card/60578), [<span class="fg-survivor">Hidden Shelter</span>](/card/60565)), but Survivors are capable of more shenanigans.

- [<span class="fg-survivor">"Shove off!"</span>](/card/12079): It won't let you evade an enemy at a nearby location, since the enemy must currently be engaged with you. But it lets Miguel drop the evaded enemy at the nearby location, because the event resolves as if he's there. When the evasion's consequence resolves in ST.7, the "as if" still applies to you.
- [<span class="fg-survivor">Scrape By</span>](/card/12082): This won't do anything, since you would change location only in ST.6, where this is played. By ST.7, where you apply the consequences, you are already back. (e.g. You can't change where you drop off enemies on evasion, unlike with [<span class="fg-survivor">"Shove off!"</span>](/card/12079).)
- [<span class="fg-survivor">"Look what I found!"</span>](/card/12078): You can suddenly discover 2 clues at a nearby location after failing an investigation at your current location.
- [<span class="fg-survivor">Field Dressing</span>](/card/60571): You can play it when other investigators defeat a ***Creature*** or ***Monster*** at a nearby location. You distribute the 3 damage of healing at that location.
- [<span class="fg-survivor">Respite</span>](/card/60577): Allows you to play it even while you are engaged with an enemy, by choosing a nearby location with no enemies. Though you'll take an AoO, so that's probably not worth doing. If you can't play it because an Aloof enemy is watching you, then it might be a good idea.

[<span class="fg-survivor">Guerrilla Tactics</span>](/card/60564) / [<span class="fg-survivor">Guerrilla Tactics (2)</span>](/card/60576): Ugh... brace yourself. First, let's see the regular things these do:

- **Fight** an enemy you are engaged with right now.
- **Evade** an enemy you are engaged with right now.
- Whether or not you are currently engaged with something, you use the "as if" to **Fight** an enemy at a connecting location.
- Whether or not you are currently engaged with something, you use the "as if" to **Evade** an enemy at a connecting location.
- You cannot use it to **Fight** or **Evade** an Aloof enemy at your current location that you are not engaged with, since you are not using the "as if".
- You can use it to **Fight** or **Evade** an Aloof enemy at a connecting location, because the "as if" gives you the engagement.

So the easiest way to think about it is to apply the "as if" of [<span class="fg-survivor">Miguel's Knapsack</span>](/card/60552) first, before you do the above. The first 2 points, about an enemy you are engaged with, still stay true: since it is in your threat area, it goes with you. When you decide to use the "as if" on [<span class="fg-survivor">Guerrilla Tactics</span>](/card/60564) / [<span class="fg-survivor">Guerrilla Tactics (2)</span>](/card/60576), you can then extend the range one location further. If you understand this correctly, you should now see that when the Knapsack is used, you can Fight or Evade an Aloof enemy 2 locations away, but you are no longer able to Fight or Evade an Aloof enemy 1 location away (which you could if you didn't use the Knapsack).
